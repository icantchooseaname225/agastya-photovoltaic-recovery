## Official guidance checked

The DST documents page lists an [Activity Guidebook for NCSC 2026–27](https://dst.gov.in/documents/documents). A separate DST reports-page entry dated 31 March 2026 is labelled a **draft** ([reports listing](https://www.dst.gov.in/documents/reports?page=1)). DST's [2025–26 annual report](https://dst.gov.in/sites/default/files/DST%20AR%20%202025-26%20ENG_Web.pdf) identifies the 2026–27 focal theme as “Science and Innovation for Sustainability.” Searchable text in the [guidebook PDF](https://dst.gov.in/sites/default/files/Activity-Guidebook-for-NCSC-2026-27_0.pdf) says to keep real-time, dated handwritten observations/data in the project book; it says printed raw data or rewriting those raw observations into another book is not acceptable for evaluation. It suggests a 150–250 word abstract and practice explaining the work in 3–5 minutes. The guidebook also frames NCSC work around a student-selected local problem, a team/guide, hypothesis and field research. These are planning references; confirm the final rules with the student's district/state coordinator.

The accessible official material did **not** confirm poster requirement/count/dimensions, mounting, copy count, report forms, local deadline, presentation time limit, digital-media rules or working-model requirements for this student's event. Do not convert an unconfirmed item into “mandatory.”

## Category C — must physically make or prepare

**No process hardware is essential or justified now.** The student should maintain the original dated handwritten project notebook and prepare to explain the real work in their own words.

Optional, only if the event allows a small display and the student wants a tangible teaching aid:

| Item | Decision | Materials/effort | What it demonstrates | What it does not demonstrate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper-token material-flow board | Optional; decide after coordinator confirms display rules | Reuse paper/card, markers and tape; about 45–90 minutes; routine adult/teacher check | A sample ID follows through numbered streams and each mass must be accounted for | It is not a separator, sample, experiment, assay or silver-recovery result |

If there is no suitable physical display, use the current slide deck and the transparent literature arithmetic on a computer, if allowed. Label every drawing “conceptual” and the model “accounting demonstration.”

Do not use acids, high-voltage equipment, hot knives, panel cutting/crushing, milling, dust generation or live chemical treatment as a school demonstration. Any genuine process test needs a qualified facility, written risk review, permissions and appropriate adult supervision.

## Category D — digital only unless rules say otherwise

- Source evidence register, full evidence database and literature notes.
- Raw EYE images/manifests, virtual simulation CSVs and model code.
- Detailed engineering specifications, missing-input tables, calculations and validation results.
- Speaker notes, slide evidence register, visual register and judge-question bank.
- Software/environment/reproducibility notes and the full file inventory.
- Long technical reports, original thesis/abstract and historical logbook scans/entries.

Keep shortcuts to these files on the laptop used for preparation; do not print large technical records for a small display.

## Category E — do not make or print

- Do not print four A1 posters simply because four PDFs exist.
- Do not produce duplicate summary sheets, multiple competing “final” decks or decorative panels with no science content.
- Do not make unverified recovery/grade/purity graphs or fill missing data with zeros.
- Do not build a high-voltage separator, thermal delamination line, crusher or chemical rig as a display prop.
- Do not use a rendering or stock photo that implies an AGASTYA prototype exists.
- Do not add a QR code without a stable, accessible destination approved for public sharing.
- Do not buy hardware or pay for software before sample, method, supervision, budget and question are defined.

## Category G — confirm with organiser

Ask the coordinator and record the answer/date for each:

- Event level, category, student eligibility, team size, language and guide requirements.
- Current final guidebook and local/district/state additions.
- Submission opening/closing dates, report form, abstract format/word count and required signatures.
- Whether a poster/display is required; count, finished dimensions, orientation, board size, mounting method and colour/copy rules.
- Presentation time, question time, digital presentation/device/internet rules.
- Whether a working model is required/optional; permitted materials, electrical limits, safety review and adult-supervision rules.
- Project-book format and whether original dated handwritten entries must be shown at evaluation.
- Permission/ethics for surveys, interviews, photographs, waste samples, lab access and public display of names/photos.
