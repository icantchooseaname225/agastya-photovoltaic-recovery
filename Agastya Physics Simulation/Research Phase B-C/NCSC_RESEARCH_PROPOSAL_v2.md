# NCSC concept proposal — draft, pending local validation

## Working title

Can a transparent material-balance plan make solar-panel silver-recovery claims testable?

## Status

This is a proposal concept, not a submission or completed community study. No local panel-waste source, student team, guide, facility or sample access is documented. Before submission, students must identify a specific neighborhood issue and do student-led field or analytical work under a guide.

## Research question and method

For a defined local or safely obtained PV sample, what measurements are required to determine whether mechanical size separation partitions silver-bearing material usefully? First confirm whether end-of-life PV panels or cell scraps are present locally and how they are handled. If a guide and coordinator approve a safe study, obtain documented sample portions through an authorized facility. Record source, dimensions and mass; compare size fractions; weigh every stream; and commission qualified assays with sample IDs, blanks and duplicates. Calculate material yield, Ag grade, Ag recovery and residual separately. Without sample or assay access, reframe the work as a local waste-handling survey and evidence review; do not call it a recovery experiment.

## Draft abstract for student and guide revision

End-of-life crystalline-silicon solar panels contain recoverable materials, but published recovery percentages use different feeds and measurements. This study develops a transparent plan for checking whether mechanical size separation could concentrate silver-bearing material from a defined photovoltaic waste sample. We reviewed peer-reviewed studies on crush-and-sieve separation, electrostatic separation, flotation and leaching, then separated reported silver recovery, product grade and particle-size partition. A reproducible accounting model was tested with 28 unit tests and two literature-based 1 kg inventory scenarios. The calculations show that reference silver content can be converted to a normalized inventory, but no AGASTYA process recovery can be calculated without weighed output streams and assays. Published electrostatic work reports co-concentration of silver, aluminium and silicon but does not provide an accessible mass-based silver recovery; other routes report results on different feed bases and cannot be directly ranked. No AGASTYA physical sample, assay or solver run is claimed. The next student-led step is to confirm a local PV-waste problem and, with an approved facility, measure the mass and assay of each size fraction. The present work supplies a testable measurement framework, not evidence of silver recovered by AGASTYA.

The abstract describes completed desk research and proposes future local work. Students must revise it to match their own local methods and findings. Students should own the local question, sample choice, notebook and interpretation. A guide must approve safety and any laboratory work.
