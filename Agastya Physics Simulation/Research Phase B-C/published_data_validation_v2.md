# Published-data reproduction and arithmetic checks

These are literature checks, not AGASTYA results.

## Saffarian et al. (2026), tap-water flotation table

The study used prepared, delaminated and ground end-of-life cell material. Its mean row reports 110 g feed at 0.75 wt% Ag, 3.11 g concentrate at 24.0 wt% Ag and 106.74 g tail at 0.019 wt% Ag. The authors report 97.55% mean Ag recovery and 32.2× mean upgrade for three tap-water tests.

Using the rounded mean row:
- Feed Ag = 110.00 × 0.75% = 0.8250 g.
- Concentrate Ag = 3.11 × 24.0% = 0.7464 g.
- Tail Ag = 106.74 × 0.019% = 0.0202806 g.
- Tail-depletion recovery = 100 × (1 − 0.0202806/0.8250) = 97.5417%, close to the reported 97.55%.
- Upgrade from mean assays = 24.0/0.75 = 32.0×, close to reported 32.2×.
- Three-stream Ag closure from rounded mean figures = 92.9304%, leaving 0.0583194 g mean-row residual.
- Total mass residual from rounded mean figures = 0.15 g.

A ratio of means differs from a mean of per-test calculations. This check does not prove a source error; it shows rounded mean values alone cannot recreate every per-test balance. Attribute 97.55% to the authors and preserve the study feed basis and method.

Citation: Saffarian, Galvin & Firouzi, Minerals Engineering 242 (2026), 110189, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mineng.2026.110189.

## Separate inventory arithmetic

A 300 ppm composition scenario yields 0.300 g Ag per normalized 1 kg. Dias et al.'s 630 g/t reported average yields 0.630 g per normalized 1 kg only as arithmetic on that study-specific basis. These are not a measured interval, pooled estimate or AGASTYA assay. Equations are in published_data_validation_v2.csv.

The 2023 electrostatic study by de Souza and Veit reports Ag, Al and Si co-concentration, but its accessible abstract does not provide mass-weighted Ag recovery or product grade. Its 49.22% value is the pre-separator smallest size fraction. The separate Li et al. study reports silicon, not silver, recovery. The 2024 mechanical study's 94.12% is Ag partition to a <0.25 mm fraction for its feed, not product grade or an AGASTYA forecast.
