# India solar-PV market, regulatory and commercial snapshot

**Checked:** 19 November 2026. Market context only, not legal advice or AGASTYA business case.

## Waste volume: projections, not measured inventory

CEEW's 2025 roadmap estimates about 146 kt cumulative Indian solar waste through end-2024 and models roughly 11,221 kt by 2047, 92% c-Si. It uses government deployment data to 2024, projected additions, modeled early/mid-life and end-of-life losses, technology shares and material intensities. It assumes 100% collection for capacity planning; this is not a claim that the volume is currently collected or available.

An earlier MNRE/CEEW analysis describes 340 kt by 2030 from installed capacity and about 600 kt including projected additions FY24–FY30. Scopes differ. Keep these as labeled model scenarios, not measured national tonnage or feed contracts.

## Regulation

The Ministry's rules index lists E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 and later amendments. Chapter V addresses solar PV modules/panels/cells. The 2022 text includes producer/manufacturer registration, storage of PV waste generated up to 2034–2035 under CPCB guidance and annual returns through that period. It states standard EPR recycling targets do not apply to PV waste. This is not a blanket exemption for other constituents, residues, facilities, transport or worker safety.

A school prototype or supervised characterization does not make AGASTYA a registered recycler. Confirm consolidated rules and facility obligations with the host before transfer or processing.

## India commercial economics context

CEEW's 2025 analysis models full-module mechanical and chemical recycling. It reports about ₹10,230/t loss for mechanical and ₹12,341/t loss for chemical in its reference scenarios; modeled recurring costs about ₹40,132/t and ₹49,067/t. Inputs include capacity, machinery, labor, energy, maintenance, yield, purity and prices. These are not AGASTYA estimates, but show why favorable Ag recovery alone does not prove profitability.

That model reports ₹4,253/t silver benefit on its chemical route. The 2026 flotation paper's introduction cites a generic module Ag-content range of 300–500 g/t. Multiplying 0.3–0.5 kg/t by IBJA's 19-Nov-2026 AM 999 rate of ₹161,575/kg gives calculated gross contained silver value ₹48,473–₹80,788/t before recovery, buyer discount, feed cost, transport, process, assay, waste, equipment or taxes. These are not directly comparable: module generation, basis, product, recovery and system boundaries differ. The divergence is a reason to assay an actual panel, not select the larger number.

IBJA is an ex-GST 999 reference, not an offer for a mixed concentrate. AGASTYA has no verified buyer, feed contract, recovery, throughput, reagent price or equipment quote.

## Verifiable lab cost

IIT Delhi CRF's posted 2026 list gives ₹1,000 +18% GST per external-academic solid ICP-MS sample and ₹1,800 +18% for one FESEM-EDX option. The ICP-MS page gives generic 100–500 mg solid guidance for digestion-required material, says sample preparation is normally the user's responsibility, requests prior discussion and says unapproved submissions will not be entertained. External users must register and obtain approval before booking/payment. ₹1,180 is one listed instrument fee including GST, not a full PV assay quote or matrix validation. No PV feed acceptance is established.

No robot, fixture, hot knife/roller, ECS, flotation cell, water treatment or dust-control quote was verified. No CAPEX total is supportable.

## Sensitivity inputs and status

| Input | Status | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Ag content per actual module | Unknown | Representative assay and construction record |
| Stage 2/3 mass and Ag retention | Unknown | Supervised measured balances |
| Product grade/recovery/buyer payables | Unknown | Matched-feed assay and receiver quote |
| Feed acquisition/transport | Unknown | Contract and route-specific quote |
| Robot/thermal/separation CAPEX | Unknown | Supplier offers, installation/site/guarding cost |
| Reagent price/dose basis | Unknown | Delivered India quote and study clarification |
| Water, energy, labor, maintenance | Unknown | Metered trial and current tariff/contract |
| Residue treatment | Unknown | Host classification and disposal quote |

Stage 4 code varies recovery mathematically; these values are sensitivity axes, not AGASTYA results. Reagent cost remains symbolic.

## Commercial interpretation

A defensible model also needs reverse logistics, marketable glass/aluminium/copper fractions, licensed downstream partner access and buyer acceptance for a defined product. AGASTYA remains a research/pilot route with open measurements, not a demonstrated profitable system.
