This file's size-classification, ECS, assay, or hand-off descriptions are prior route research, not the current stage definition. Treat size reduction/classification as possible feed preparation; assay and mass accounting validate the stage. See [Stage 4 architecture and route decision](STAGE4_ARCHITECTURE_AND_ROUTE_DECISION_2026-10-16.md).

**Review date:** 7 October 2026  
**Scope:** Stress-test the thesis proposal (rotating eddy-current separator with optional electrostatic polishing) against the current Agastya material path and compare feasible alternatives.  
**Status:** Desk review. No Stage 4 sample, equipment, partner access, assay, throughput, safety review or cost quote has been verified.

## Decision

The earlier thesis eddy-current model is **not ready to build or describe as a silver-removal process**. Current Stage 4 is silver removal/recovery. The evidence-based Stage 3 output is front glass plus a cell/EVA/backsheet laminate; the proposed roller peel is unvalidated. Even after cell liberation, printed silver may remain attached to silicon or polymer. An eddy-current drum can alter the trajectory of a conductive composite particle, but that alone does not show that silver has been removed or recovered.

Use the corrected Stage 4 objective: **remove/recover silver from Stage 3-derived cell-bearing material**. Define the product form, actual feed state, route-specific preparation, assay/QC, facility and receiver requirements before choosing equipment. A dry mixed fraction may be an intermediate, but it is not the silver-removal result by itself.

The best current baseline is a supervised, controlled liberation and multi-cut size-classification study, conditional on a safe, approved source of eligible feed. This is not yet a machine-selection or purchase decision. If feed liberation, dust controls, assay, or a receiver cannot be arranged, the most defensible system alternative is to stop the mobile line at Stage 3 and hand the laminate to a qualified partner that has an established downstream route.

## Problems in the proposed model

| Problem | Why it affects the design | Severity | Required correction |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Stage 4 inlet is not defined | Stage 3 hot-knife work supports a glass/cell/EVA/backsheet laminate. It does not produce free metal particles or prove EVA removal. | Critical | Document the real output state, moisture, mass, particle sizes, polymer/glass carryover and degree of liberation before choosing Stage 4 equipment. |
| “Conductive” is being treated as “separable silver” | ECS sorts particles according to induced-current response and trajectory. A silicon fragment carrying a printed Ag trace may move as one composite; ECS does not separate Ag from Si or distinguish Ag from Al/Cu by conductivity alone. | Critical | Define which particle is expected to move and what target assay changes. Measure Ag in feed and every output fraction; report grade, recovery and enrichment separately. |
| The drum's feed window is missing | ECS response depends on particle size, shape, mass, conductivity, rotor/field frequency, speed, splitter and feed presentation. Fine, light, thin or irregular particles can have poor or variable trajectories. | High | Obtain the candidate separator's accepted size/shape envelope and test only eligible, size-classified feed at a supervised facility. Do not transfer fixed-frequency EDX results to a rotary drum. |
| Optional electrostatic “polishing” is underspecified | Corona/roll electrostatic separators require dry, liberated, dust-controlled material in a defined particle-size range and use high voltage. They separate by charge/conductivity response; that does not automatically mean Ag purity. | High | Treat it as a separate lab comparator, identify the exact machine and feed acceptance limits, and compare assay results with size classification alone. |
| No product or receiver specification | “Small concentrated silicon-silver fraction” has no minimum grade, mass, moisture, contaminant limit, packaging, assay or receiving terms. A research-enriched fraction may have no practical outlet. | Critical | Ask a named receiver for its feed acceptance and sample/assay requirements before optimizing the process. Until then use “candidate mixed fraction.” |
| Liberation step and safety boundary are absent | Grinding or crushing changes the process and creates sharp silicon/glass and fine dust. Over-crushing can contaminate fines and make separation harder. | Critical | Make liberation an explicit, guarded and facility-approved operation. No crushing or screening trial until a qualified supervisor, containment and waste route are recorded. |
| The thesis says “no consumables” | A dry process may avoid process reagents, but still needs power, sample containers/labels, screen cleaning, dust collection/filter service, PPE and maintenance. | Medium | Say “no process chemicals in the onboard route” if that boundary remains true; budget recurring supplies and maintenance separately. |
| No whole-line throughput, energy or cost basis | Separator-only research figures omit Stage 3, preparation, dust management, assay, cleaning, packaging and transport. Stage 3's theoretical daily capacity is not full-line capacity. | High | Model the line bottleneck as the minimum measured capacity of each operation. Meter energy by operation and use actual quotes, facility fees and assay charges. |
| Evidence is being conflated across machines and feeds | Fixed-frequency EDX, rotary ECS, corona separators and laboratory flotation have different mechanisms and feed windows. Published grade/recovery values belong to their own samples and boundaries. | High | Keep method-specific evidence separate; label PV studies by feed preparation, machine, size range and whether results describe grade or recovery. |

## Alternatives compared

| Alternative | Fit and evidence | Main limitations | Agastya decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 3 hand-off to qualified recycler** | Uses Agastya for identification, dismantling, glass/laminate separation and traceability; partner carries out delamination and metal recovery with suitable infrastructure. | Partner and acceptance terms must be found; less downstream processing stays on the vehicle. | **Best fallback / lowest technical risk.** Do this if feed, assay or controls for a Stage 4 pilot are unavailable. |
| **Controlled liberation + multi-cut sieving** | PV-specific crush-and-sieve literature reports silver-bearing material concentrated in fines. Juyo Salazar et al. report 94.12% of Ag directed to a <0.25 mm size fraction; this is Ag distribution/recovery into that fraction, not 94.12 wt% Ag product. | Requires a real liberation step, dust control, representative assay and material balance. Fine fraction can contain glass, silicon and polymer; high recovery can come with low product grade. | **Best baseline research route**, only using eligible feed at an approved facility. Compare at least two size cuts by mass and assay. |
| **Gas-solid fluidized-bed shape/density separation** | Feng et al. (2023) report >80% recovery/concentration of liberated solar-cell particles in coarse 2–4 mm and >4 mm cuts. It is a possible dry cell-versus-glass operation after liberation. | Their best liberation used 500 °C pyrolysis for 30 minutes; that is outside Agastya's current mobile/no-fume boundary. The separator does not extract silver from the cell. | **Partner-lab comparator** if a partner can provide already liberated, coarse feed; not an onboard build candidate now. |
| **Corona electrostatic drum/roll** | Dry conductor/non-conductor separation can handle fine, liberated feed. A 2026 MSc study on controlled PV fractions reports >80% recovery across glass, Si and metallic classes and about 53% Si grade for one fine-size condition; the coarser size class needed its own optimization. | Thesis-level evidence; result is not an Ag-specific recovery figure. Requires high voltage, dry and dust-controlled feed, narrow size preparation and specialized equipment. | **Second-choice lab comparator**, if a supervised unit and suitable feed are available. Do not copy reported voltage/speed as Agastya settings. |
| **Fixed-frequency EDX / electrodynamic separation** | PV-specific work reports >85% grade and recovery for prepared 1–3 mm surrogate Si/Al and CdTe/Al mixtures at about 10 kg/h; reported energy is ~75 kWh/t for that sorting operation. | Specialized stationary field system; study used prepared surrogates, not Agastya's laminate or an ordinary rotating ECS. Significant energy for small-scale throughput. | **Research benchmark only.** Useful to compare mechanism; not a proxy for the thesis drum. |
| **High-voltage pulse liberation** | Can liberate and enrich PV fractions. Zhao et al. report a clear grade/recovery trade-off: about 3.08× Ag enrichment at 54.07% recovery under one condition, versus 1.67× at 89.41% recovery under another. | Specialist high-voltage comminution equipment and safety controls; outcome is mixed fraction, not pure Ag. | **External-lab alternative** only if Ag pre-concentration remains the priority and partner access exists. |
| **Froth flotation** | A 2026 lab study reports silver-specific upgrading after delamination and grinding; rougher test reported 97.55% Ag recovery and 32.2× upgrade, while rougher-cleaner tests reported ~47 wt% Ag at 86.5% recovery. | Water, reagents, prepared cell feed, process water/waste handling and lab equipment. Not dry or mobile; one recent study. | **Partner/refiner benchmark**, not an Agastya onboard method. |
| **Conventional rotating ECS** | Mature at separating suitable free non-ferrous metal particles from non-metallic material. | Does not separate Ag from Si, Ag from Al/Cu, or metal traces from a still-bonded composite. Performance falls outside suitable size/shape/feed windows. | **Demote from core.** Retain only if assay and feed characterization show a coarse liberated conductor fraction for which it improves the result. |

## Recommended next sequence

1. **Correct the claim and block diagram.** Show the unresolved cell/EVA/backsheet liberation step between Stage 3 and any Stage 4 separator. Name the output a mixed, assay-defined fraction, not silver.
2. **Resolve the input.** Confirm whether Agastya has access to a traceable c-Si sample and whether any partner can prepare it safely. Record feed state, dry mass, size distribution and visible polymer/glass carryover.
3. **Find the receiving and assay boundary.** Request material acceptance rules from a recycler/refiner and confirm an assay method (XRF screening only where suitable; qualified lab method if needed). Confirm how many samples and what preparation are required.
4. **Compare on equivalent feed.** If gates pass, compare multi-cut sieving alone with one candidate separator on equivalent aliquots. Retain and assay every stream, including dust/cleanup residues where the facility protocol permits. Record mass closure, Ag grade, Ag recovery, enrichment, uncertainty, energy, time and consumables.
5. **Use a decision rule.** Keep a separator only if it gives repeatable assay-confirmed improvement over sieving alone and the receiver accepts the resulting fraction. Otherwise stop at size classification or use the partner hand-off route.
6. **Cost after the process boundary is fixed.** Price only the selected operation using real supplier/facility quotes. Include preparation, enclosure/dust extraction, sieve set, containers, scales, power meter, assay, PPE/filter upkeep, transport, waste, labour and service. No total cost, throughput or recovered-material value can be responsibly quoted yet.

## Cost and feasibility implications

No comparative capital-cost estimate is defensible from the current evidence: no vendor model, equipment envelope, school/lab fee, dust-control specification, feed rate or quote is known. The practical cost ranking is therefore qualitative:

- **Lowest expected Agastya capital commitment:** partner hand-off / partner-run processing (per-batch fee and transport unknown).
- **Lowest-complexity lab baseline:** sieves, balance, contained sample handling and assay access; cost still depends on facility and analysis quotes.
- **Higher integration burden:** corona electrostatic or ECS/EDX (specialized machine, guarded feed handling, controls, maintenance and dust management).
- **Outside current vehicle boundary:** high-voltage pulse, pyrolysis, flotation and chemical extraction (specialist facility, safety/environmental controls, and in some cases reagents/waste treatment).

Do not substitute hobby-scale online prices for quotes. Compare cost per accepted kilogram only after measuring mass yield, assay, rejected streams, facility fee, labour, transport and residual management.

## Literature and evidence notes

1. Feng, Y. et al. (2023). “A promising method for the liberation and separation of solar cells from damaged crystalline silicon photovoltaic modules.” *Solar Energy Materials and Solar Cells*, 262, 112553. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solmat.2023.112553). The fluidized-bed step separates liberated cells and glass by shape/density; upstream liberation in the study relied on pyrolysis.
2. IEA PVPS Task 12 / EPRI (2024). *Advances in PV Module Recycling: Literature Review and Update to Empirical Life Cycle Inventory Data and Patent Review*. [Report PDF](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/IEA-PVPS-Report-EPRI-PV-Recycling-T12-28-2024.pdf). The report notes a commercial pure-mechanical route whose output did not recover silicon and silver because much remained encapsulated in polymers.
3. Smith, Y.R., Nagel, J.R. & Rajamani, R.K. (2019). “Eddy current separation for recovery of non-ferrous metallic particles: A comprehensive review.” *Minerals Engineering*, 133, 149–159. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mineng.2018.12.025). Reviews rotary ECS strengths and geometry/feed limitations.
4. Juyo Salazar, R.B. et al. (2024). “Streamlined process with a sustainable approach for photovoltaic module recycling.” *Sustainable Materials and Technologies*, 41, e01047. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.susmat.2024.e01047). Reports size-fraction distribution, including Ag in the <0.25 mm fraction; fraction distribution is not product grade.
5. Mbam, N.M. (2026). *Variant Study of Dry Beneficiation Methods for PV Module Cell Fractions*. MSc thesis, Montanuniversität Leoben. [Repository / DOI](https://doi.org/10.34901/mul.pub.2026.194). Evidence is thesis-level and settings belong to the study apparatus.
6. Zhao, Y. et al. (2020). “Liberation and recovery of metals from waste photovoltaic modules by high-voltage pulse crushing.” *Journal of Cleaner Production*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2020.120442).
7. Saffarian, H., Galvin, K.P. & Firouzi, M. (2026). “Recovery of silver from end-of-life crystalline silicon solar cells by froth flotation.” *Minerals Engineering*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mineng.2026.110189).
8. hamos GmbH, “Electrostatic separator for metal separation from photovoltaic modules.” [Manufacturer feed requirements](https://hamos.com/en/recycling-plants/electrostatic-separator-for-metal-separation-from-photovoltaic-modules/). Manufacturer states feed should be completely liberated, dry, largely dust-free and 100 μm–8 mm; treat as supplier guidance, not independent performance validation.

## Conclusion

Stage 4's silver-removal function is fixed, but the current evidence does not select a method. Controlled size classification may prepare or preconcentrate feed; ECS may sort eligible liberated particles; flotation or chemical/electrochemical routes may remove or concentrate Ag under specialist conditions. The next decision is to characterize the Stage 3 output, state the intended product form, and compare methods at a qualified facility. No route is approved or validated for AGASTYA.
