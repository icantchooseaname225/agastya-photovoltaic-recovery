# Stage 4 architecture and route decision

**Date:** 16 October 2026  
**Current role:** silver removal/recovery  
**Status:** function fixed; route, feed interface, product form and facility remain open.

## Corrected process sequence

Stage 1 inspects/routes modules. Stage 2 performs manual mechanical preparation. Stage 3 thermally delaminates prepared c-Si modules; the working hot-knife reference separates front glass and produces a cell/EVA/backsheet laminate. Stage 4 removes or recovers silver from Stage 3-derived cell-bearing material, with additional feed preparation only when the selected route requires it. See [AGASTYA_ARCHITECTURE.md](../../AGASTYA_ARCHITECTURE.md).

Prior Phase D/E files described Stage 4 as sampling, assay and accounting. Prior Stage 4 notes then investigated dry comminution/classification and ECS for a mixed solid hand-off. Those definitions are superseded. Assay/accounting remains necessary cross-stage validation; comminution, sieving and electrostatic separation may be feed preparation or candidate operations, but they do not by themselves establish silver removal.

## Stage 3 to Stage 4 interface

The baseline hot-knife output still contains EVA and backsheet with the cell-bearing material. The Stage 4 feed cannot be called EVA-free, liberated silver, or assay-ready solely because Stage 3 separated glass. The required Stage 4 input must be specified after a route is selected:

- feed state and mass basis;
- whether additional encapsulant/backsheet removal or cell liberation is required;
- particle size, moisture, and contaminant envelope;
- desired silver-bearing output form (concentrate, solution, or recovered solid);
- receiver requirements and permitted residuals;
- qualified facility, supervision, safety controls, and waste route.

## Candidate route categories

Existing literature sources in this folder support comparison, not immediate construction or operation:

| Route category | Stage 4 relevance | Key boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical liberation, milling and size classification | Can prepare or preconcentrate a silver-bearing fraction for a downstream removal step. | Size partition is not silver extraction or product purity; feed-specific mass and Ag assays are required. |
| Electrostatic / eddy-current separation | May partition an eligible liberated particle class as a physical separation step. | Does not automatically remove printed Ag from a composite cell fragment; should not be called a silver-removal result without assay evidence. |
| Froth flotation | Recent lab literature reports Ag-specific beneficiation on prepared, delaminated and ground cell feed. | Wet route with reagents and specialist equipment; result is study-specific and needs qualified facility/waste controls. |
| Chemical or electrochemical extraction | Can transfer silver from prepared feed into solution or another product form. | Requires route-specific process development, chemical controls, validated assay, waste treatment and qualified supervision; no student improvisation. |
| External refiner process | A qualified receiver can perform downstream silver removal/recovery if it accepts the actual feed. | Feed acceptance, fees, product form and recovery evidence must be obtained in writing. |

## Decision gate

Do not select a method from a published recovery percentage alone. First characterize the actual Stage 3 output and decide what “silver removal” must produce. Then compare candidate routes for Ag balance, material losses, process hazards, facilities, waste, costs, assay QA/QC, and receiver acceptance. Route-specific studies should record feed Ag, all product/residue streams, mass closure, Ag grade, Ag recovery, enrichment, product purity where relevant, uncertainty, time, energy and cost.

Until those gates pass, Stage 4 remains a defined objective with an open method—not a completed operation. No AGASTYA silver-removal trial, assay, product, recovery, purity, cost, or environmental result is claimed.
