## Stage 3 findings

A common glass/backsheet c-Si module contains front glass, encapsulant (often EVA, but POE/EPE/PVB and other constructions occur), silicon cells with silver-bearing metallization and copper ribbons, rear encapsulant and polymer backsheet. Frame, cables and junction box are Stage 2 items. The hot knife is intended to separate front glass at/near the glass/encapsulant interface. EVA remains on/around the cell-bearing sheet; cells are not liberated, silver fingers are not detached from silicon, the backsheet is not removed, and copper ribbons remain unless separately cut/detached. The Al frame has already been removed; any Al in this feed is residual fragments/contamination.

IEA PVPS has demonstrated hot-knife glass/laminate separation as a recycling operation, outputting sheet glass and a cell/EVA sheet for downstream processing. This supports the process class, not Agastya reliability, yield, settings or safety. Reliable removal on Agastya is **unestablished**; current Stage 3 does **not** remove all encapsulant or all module layers. Glass/glass modules retain rear glass; removing front glass leaves rear glass, encapsulant and cells together. Layer thickness, encapsulant formulation, age/weathering and construction can change heat flow, adhesion and failure path; these need feed-specific measurements.

| Candidate input/operation | Output and interface separated | What remains bonded / contamination | Required validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepared glass/backsheet module; current guarded hot knife | Front glass sheet separated near glass/encapsulant interface; cell-bearing laminate | EVA/cells/rear encapsulant/backsheet remain; possible glass/EVA residue, cracks or ribbon damage | Instrumented coupons; temperature, speed, force/peel, energy, breakage, carryover, mass balance and safety. |
| Glass/glass module; front-side hot knife | Front glass may separate | Rear glass remains bonded through encapsulant to cells; fragments possible | Identify lay-up and validate separately. |
| Staged backsheet removal plus mechanical sanding/abrasion | Backsheet removed first; cell/EVA/Cu fraction or powder | Polymer, Si fines, Cu and abrasion dust may remain | PSD/liberation, polymer carryover, dust and Ag/Cu/Al partition. |
| Milling/crush and size classification | Broken laminate divided into size fractions; Ag-bearing fines may enrich | Mixed Si/Ag/Cu/polymer/glass, broad sizes | Weigh/assay every fraction; not Ag-selective. |
| Elevated-temperature thermal/oxidative treatment | Published studies report more complete polymer removal at higher temperatures/times than interface hot knife | Fumes/residue, energy, oxidation and downstream mixed cell/metal fraction | Separate facility-scale option; emissions, energy, material quality and safety review. Not a drop-in setting. |
| Pressurized hydrothermal treatment | Hot water separates glass/interlayer for subsequent peeling | Pressure-vessel and water management; does not recover Ag or necessarily liberate cells | Specialized facility and independent validation. |
| Solvent/ultrasound | Recent comparison found incomplete polymer removal under its toluene/ultrasound conditions; thermal route differed | Solvent residues/recovery/waste; residual polymer | Not preferred for low-hazard/no-reagent objective; facility study only. |

Keep Stage 3’s objective narrow: remove front glass while retaining traceable laminate. If Stage 4 needs liberated cell fragments, investigate separate Stage 4 mechanical feed preparation or separately validated Stage 3B. Cleaner glass does not itself demonstrate improved Ag recovery; recovery requires retaining, liberating, concentrating and assaying all metal-bearing streams.

## Stage 4 Step 4A/4B findings

| Method | Assessment |
|---|---|
| 4A eddy-current separation (ECS) | Expected Stage 3 feed has bonded Cu ribbons/metallization, not free conductive particles. ECS cannot detach bonded layers and is not Ag-selective; Cu, Al and Ag are conductors. Omit from base route. Test only if liberated coarse Cu/Al is found and paired assays show benefit without unacceptable Ag loss. |
| Visible cables/ribbons/loose fragments | Manual/mechanical removal is the simplest initial 4A screen. Weigh and assay removed and retained streams; it cannot remove bonded metallization. |
| 4B mechanical liberation and size classification | Leading low-cost, no-added-reagent first trial after Stage 3. Published Ag partition to fines is feed-specific and not grade/recovery. Measure PSD/liberation and Ag/Cu/Al in each fraction. |
| 4B dry corona electrostatic separation | Candidate on liberated/classified feed. Studies show co-concentration, not transferable Ag-only selectivity. Weigh and assay all products. |
| Wet froth flotation | 2026 study used already-delaminated/ground feed, water/air and AEROFLOAT 242 frother plus AEROPHINE 3418A collector. Physical beneficiation with reagents, not strict no-reagent or silver metal; consider only if separately accepted with waste controls. |
| Density/conductivity-only methods | No Ag-specific result on Agastya feed; composite/bonded particles limit selectivity. Low priority before liberation/assays. |
| Chemical dissolution/refining | Distinct from physical concentration and outside present low-hazard objective. |

Sim et al. report 70%/80% of silver in the finest fraction for two prepared feed categories; without fine mass yield this is not concentrate grade. de Souza and Veit report Ag/Si/Al co-concentration by electrostatic processing, not transferable Agastya mass recovery/grade. Yang’s accessible “silver separation efficiency” is not established as mass-based recovery and grade. Saffarian et al. (2026) report study-specific flotation on prepared feed; their rougher-cleaner product was about 46.7 wt% Ag with lower recovery than rougher-only treatment, and is a concentrate rather than metal.

Provisional route for owner review: Stage 2 manual preparation → current Stage 3 glass removal → Stage 4 liberation/classification → dry corona-electrostatic partition with all streams weighed/assayed. Omit 4A unless liberation proves a useful conductor stream. Flotation is a later reagent/water benchmark only. If 4A loses fraction p of Ag, downstream gain must exceed 1/(1-p): 10% Ag loss requires >11.1% Stage 4B recovery gain. No Stage 3 temperature-to-Ag-retention model is assumed.
