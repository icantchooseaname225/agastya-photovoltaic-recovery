# Agastya Project Logbook: Day 11

**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Record status:** expanded literature review and engineering feasibility assessment only. No Stage 4 machine, PV feed preparation, or separation trial is claimed. Project-day numbering records work sequence, not one distinct calendar date per entry.

## Objective

Assess whether the thesis's Stage 4 eddy-current and optional electrostatic-separation concept is technically feasible as described and compatible with Agastya's current Stage 3 output and mobile/no-on-site-chemistry boundary.

## Work completed

- Distinguished a conventional rotating-drum eddy-current separator from fixed high-frequency electrodynamic sorting (EDX/EECS); the mechanisms and workable particle-size ranges are not interchangeable.
- Reviewed PV-focused research reporting >85% grade and recovery for 1–3 mm Si/Al surrogate particle mixtures using a specialized electrodynamic system, alongside an industrial-scrap EDX demonstration. These are literature results for their own equipment/feed, not Agastya performance.
- Reviewed crush-and-sieve PV work showing silver enrichment into fine fractions and electrostatic-separation studies showing a grade/recovery trade-off. Recorded that those results depend on upstream liberation, size classification and specific apparatus.
- Compared the Stage 3 baseline output (glass plus cell/EVA/backsheet laminate) with the input assumed by Stage 4. The current laminate is not an ECS-ready particulate feed, and the proposed roller remains unvalidated.
- Wrote a feasibility verdict, architecture comparison, end-to-end material-path assessment and conditional go/no-go gates in `../Build Files and Steps/Stage 4/STAGE4_FEASIBILITY_REVIEW.md`.

## Findings and limits

The eddy-current principle is feasible for liberated conductive particles. The thesis's specific claim that a rotating drum separates silver-bearing silicon cell fragments into a valuable concentrate is not established. Fine PV fractions may need a specialized EDX unit or another size-based/electrostatic route. No Agastya Stage 4 hardware price or full-line feasibility is established because feed size, throughput, lab access and receiver requirements remain open.

The recommended next action is to inventory existing or borrowable guarded separator and assay access before making any purchase decision. For a no-spend school demonstration, use design evidence or an accurately labelled display; do not present it as a tested recycler.
